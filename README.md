This project started because I was unsatisfied with the methods of creating musical tunings used by many in the xenharmonic community. While their structured nature makes them very powerful, that same predetermined structure also has a tendency to limit what a scale's tuning could look like. I strongly believe that this project could be of use to anyone open minded who enjoys experimenting with musical tuning.

Fundimental to the method is the observation that a scale can be conceptualised as a grid (as in Grid Theory) where nodes represent notes and weighted edges represent intervals of various importance. A tuning of all notes in a scale can be reached by periodically taking the geometric mean of the results of subpaths of paths to to a all notes in that scale. Due to the inherently explosive growth of path cardinality in graphs, computing such a tuning can take a very long time. So, an arbitrary lower interval weight limit is used, which nonetheless has minimal impact on the final tuning of each note.

From an aesthetic perspective, I enjoy the fact that such a tuning will never quite be predictable - it is a 'black box' system where no interval will ever be pure and no two instances of the same interval will be tuned identically. This type of unpredictability mimics the pitch imprecision of acoustic instuments (e.g. imperfect string intonation or the effects of temperature change on brass instruments) which I find to be one of many subtle depths that such instruments have over digital ones. It occurs to me you could even tune an acoustic instrument according to one of these tunings. At it's best, this could mimic the type of tunings used in musical traditions which favour tuning by ear.

To experiment for yourself take a look at TuningMaker.exe. It should be noted that within the project, TuningMaker.cpp does not actually contain the logic of the tuning method, and should not be considered to be the primary focus of the project. In truth it was simply tacked on at the end as a (debatably) user friendly way of interacting with the Scale and PitchSpace classes. Feel free to reach out with any contributions, questions, corrections, additions, or comments.

Happy tuning!
